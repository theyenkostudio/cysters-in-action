# Design Research: Inspiration

Status: **first pass.** Based on web search results only; I haven't yet visited or screenshotted these sites. Treat the observations as leads to verify, not conclusions.

## Context: what PMOS is
PMOS (polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome) is the new name for PCOS, announced in May 2026 after a global consensus published in The Lancet. Diagnostic criteria and treatment are reported as unchanged. The name is being phased in over roughly three years, with full adoption in the 2028 international guideline update.

Design implications:
- Many visitors will still search for and think in terms of "PCOS". The site likely needs to say "PMOS (formerly PCOS)" prominently, at least for now.
- Cysters in Action is early to a name that is still new. That is a credibility opportunity (explain the change plainly) and a SEO consideration.
- Reported figures on how many patients/organisations were involved vary by source. Cite the Lancet paper or advocacy groups directly rather than commercial blogs.

## Patterns that came up repeatedly
1. **Clear navigation to resources.** Condition-based or topic-based, not organisation-based.
2. **Accessibility by design.** High contrast, not relying on colour alone, keyboard operability, plain language. Guy's and St Thomas' also kept printable formats and moved away from PDFs.
3. **Visible credibility.** Sources, reviewers and dates. Healthcare visitors are often anxious, so tone should respect that.
4. **Visible community.** Events, stories and ways to join, not just articles.
5. **Simple, interactive education.** The Allyist study suggests this beats heavy personalization.
6. **Warm but not childish.** Wandering Wombs and OvaOva show that illustration and rounded type can feel friendly. Risk: tipping into cliché "pink and flowers".

## Early direction hypotheses (to discuss, not decisions)
- **Palette:** teal/blue, purple and pink drawn from 🩵💜🩷, with a dark neutral for text. Needs a contrast check. Pale tints often fail AA for text.
- **Tone:** warm and credible, somewhere between Wandering Wombs (playful) and Endometriosis UK (established).
- **Structure:** Learn / Connect / Act as the primary navigation, with "What is PMOS (formerly PCOS)?" as the entry point.
- **Imagery:** illustration or abstract shape over stock photos of women. Avoid body-focused or stigma-adjacent imagery.

## Reference: SIHA (siha.com.au), supplied by the team
A skincare retailer, so the commerce is irrelevant. We're borrowing the **feel**, not the layout.

**Design principle (from the team):** easy on the eye. Everything should feel **intentional, curated, chosen.**

What's working in it:
- **Photography does the heavy lifting.** A full-bleed hero image, then tall portrait cards. People look real and unposed (visible tattoos, natural skin, looking off-camera), not stock-polished.
- **Whitespace and restraint.** Generous margins, one small headline, one quiet call to action (an underlined text link, not a button).
- **One typeface, one accent.** A clean sans throughout, with a single deep terracotta used for headings and the signup bar on a warm off-white background.
- **Muted, consistent colour grading** across all images, so the page feels cohesive.
- **Small details.** Uppercase eyebrow labels ("OUR BRANDS"), a simple carousel with quiet arrows, short descriptions truncated with "…".

Cautions for us:
- White text laid directly over photos can fail contrast depending on the image. We'd need a scrim or a different placement.
- The promo popup covers part of the hero. Avoid that on a health community site.
- Photography is the hard part. We need a plan for images that doesn't rely on stock clichés, and consent matters if we show real members (see [02-open-questions.md](02-open-questions.md)).
- Their palette is earthy and warm. Ours is hinted to be teal/purple/pink, so the question is how to keep the same calm with different colours.

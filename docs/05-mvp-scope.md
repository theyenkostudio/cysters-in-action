# MVP Scope (draft)

Status: **draft, pending clarification of the commitments** in [02-open-questions.md](02-open-questions.md).

## Principle
Ship the smallest thing that honestly delivers **Learn. Connect. Act.** and fulfils what's already been promised. Everything else is a later phase.

## Proposed MVP (starting point, to debate)
**Site** (one small, fast, accessible site)
- Home: who we are, why we exist, Learn/Connect/Act
- Learn: a handful of reviewed starter articles, with sources and "last reviewed" dates
- Connect: a simple entry point (join the newsletter, link to an existing community channel) rather than a built platform
- Act: how to get involved; one clear call to action
- About / Founding Circle
- Privacy, disclaimer, contact

**Newsletter**
- Signup form on the site, hosted on an off-the-shelf email tool
- Welcome email and a template for the first issue

## Deferred (post-MVP candidates)
- Hosted forum or member accounts
- Events
- Advocacy campaign tooling (petitions, surveys, story collection)
- Resource directory / clinician finder
- Anything else that "more" turns out to mean

## Tension to resolve: "application" vs. the cutting rules
We've now described this as a **community-driven application** with a photography-led identity. That pulls toward member accounts and photo sharing, which the cutting rules below would defer (accounts, moderation, user content). Two ways through:
1. **Curated first.** Launch with a photography-led site where the Founding Circle's photos and stories are curated by us. Add member uploads later.
2. **Thin community slice.** Launch with a minimal app that includes accounts and photo sharing, accepting the moderation and privacy work up front.

Not decided. Option 1 is much faster to ship, but only option 2 delivers "community" in the product itself. This needs a conversation with the colleague and the Founding Circle about what was promised.

## Cutting rules
1. If it needs accounts, moderation or health data from users, it's not MVP.
2. Prefer existing tools over custom builds.
3. Content and trust beat features.
